NATURAL LANGUAGE PROCESSING

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a field of Artificial Intelligence that focuses on enabling computers to process and understand human language.

1. Text Processing
Raw text usually needs preprocessing before it can be used by a machine learning model.

Common preprocessing steps include:
- Converting text to lowercase
- Removing unnecessary characters
- Tokenization
- Removing stop words when appropriate

2. Tokenization
Tokenization means dividing text into smaller units called tokens.

Depending on the tokenizer, tokens can represent words, subwords, or other pieces of text.

Example:
"I like Python"

Possible tokens:
"I", "like", "Python"

3. Stop Words
Stop words are common words that may carry limited information for some NLP tasks.

Examples can include words such as "the", "is", and "a".

Whether stop words should be removed depends on the task.

4. Stemming
Stemming reduces words to a simpler root-like form.

For example, words such as "playing" and "played" may be reduced to a common stem.

5. Lemmatization
Lemmatization converts words to their meaningful dictionary base form.

It generally uses more linguistic information than simple stemming.

6. Bag of Words
Bag of Words represents text using the frequency of words.

It does not consider the order of words.

7. TF-IDF
TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency.

It gives higher importance to words that are important in a document but less common across many documents.

8. Word Embeddings
Word embeddings represent words or tokens as numerical vectors.

They can capture relationships between words based on their usage in language.

9. Large Language Models
Large Language Models are neural network models trained on large amounts of text.

They can perform tasks such as text generation, summarization, question answering, and language understanding.

Important study tip:
First understand tokenization and basic text preprocessing. Then study Bag of Words and TF-IDF. After that, learn embeddings and Large Language Models.
